Study by listening. Ask when you get lost.

Lector is a free Chrome extension that reads any web page or PDF aloud in a natural voice, explains slides the way a tutor would, and answers your questions out loud when you pause it.

Made for revising, not just reading

Text-to-speech usually reads every page number and footer in a flat voice. Lector cleans the page up first and talks like a person.

Narrated slides

Each slide or section becomes a short spoken explanation. Bullets turn into sentences, formulas are said in words, and headers, footers, names and page numbers are left out. Press M to switch back to the original text.

Pause and ask

Press A and ask out loud, like “why is it like this?”. Lector answers from the page you're on, reads the answer to you, then carries on when you say “continue”.

Sections and time left

See every slide or heading with how long it takes. Untick the ones you don't need, jump to any part, and watch the time left go down.

How it works

  1. Open a page or a PDF

    Any article, lecture note or slide deck. PDF links open in Lector's own viewer.

  2. Press the Lector icon

    Or Alt+Shift+R. It starts from whatever is on screen, or right-click text and choose Lector: read from here.

  3. Listen, and ask when you're lost

    Press A, ask your question out loud, hear the answer, then say “continue”.

Set it up yourself

Lector isn't in the Chrome Web Store, so you add it yourself once. It works in Chrome, Edge and Brave.

  1. Download and unzip

    Download lector.zip and unzip it somewhere you'll keep it. Chrome runs Lector from that folder, so don't delete it.

  2. Turn on Developer mode

    Open chrome://extensions in the address bar and switch on Developer mode (top right).

  3. Load Lector

    Click Load unpacked and pick the unzipped lector folder (the one with manifest.json in it). Then pin Lector from the puzzle icon.

  4. Get a free Gemini key

    Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey, sign in, click Create API key, and copy it.

  5. Paste the key

    Right-click the Lector icon, choose Options, paste the key and click Save. Use Test voice to pick a voice.

  6. For PDFs on your computer

    On Lector's card in chrome://extensions, click Details and turn on Allow access to file URLs. Or use Open PDF in the viewer.

Do I need a key?

Reading aloud works without one. The key adds:

  • Narrated mode, the tutor-style explanations
  • Answers when you pause and ask

An Anthropic API key works too, if you'd rather use Claude.

Narration scripts are cached, so listening again costs no API calls.

Where your text goes

There is no Lector server. Here is everything that leaves your browser, and why.

Your browser

Your API key is stored only here, in chrome.storage.local.

No Lector server. Nothing goes to us.

  • Microsoft's online voice service

    The text being read, to turn it into speech.

  • Gemini or Claude, whichever you chose

    The nearby text, in Narrated mode and when you ask a question. On Gemini's free tier Google may use it to improve its models, so avoid private documents there.

  • Chrome's speech recognition (Google)

    Your spoken questions, to transcribe them.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Lector is free and open source. The Gemini key is free too; Google's free tier is plenty for studying. On the free tier Google may use what you send to improve its models, so don't use it on anything private.

What gets sent where?

Your key stays in your browser. The text being read goes to Microsoft's voice service to turn it into speech. In Narrated mode, and when you ask a question, the nearby text goes to Gemini (or Claude, if you use an Anthropic key). Spoken questions are turned into text by Chrome's speech recognition, which uses Google. Nothing goes to us; there is no server.

The voice sounds robotic

Open Options and check that Neural (Microsoft, online) is selected and pick a voice with Test voice. If the bar ever says “using Chrome's voice”, the online voice couldn't be reached; it comes back when you reload the page.

It mispronounces a word

Add it in Options under Pronunciation fixes, one per line, like Kafka = Kaf-ka.

Nothing happens when I click the icon

Chrome doesn't let extensions run on its own pages (chrome://, the Web Store) or on its built-in PDF viewer. For a PDF, click the icon and it reopens in Lector's viewer.

How do I update?

Download the new zip, replace the old folder's contents, and click the reload arrow on Lector in chrome://extensions. Your settings stay.
